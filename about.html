How it works

Sarpa shows you a real photograph of a snake from India or from North America (the USA and Canada) and asks you to name it from four options. After you answer you see why the right answer is right and why each of the other three options is not, including how they differ from the snake in the photo.

The game holds 12,290 photographs of 320 species: 162 from India and 160 from North America. Every quiz is built fresh: species are balanced, the photos you have already seen are avoided, and the harder difficulty levels fill the options with genuine look-alikes.

Play one region, or Mixed for both at once. Points depend on how hard the snake and the photo are, and Mixed quizzes score 20% more. Each region has its own daily challenge and Herpdle, and anyone can play either.

Where the photos come from

All photos are community observations on iNaturalist that reached "research grade", meaning other naturalists agreed with the species identification. They were taken in India, the United States and Canada, and every photographer chose an open Creative Commons licence.

This edition has photos from 5,047 photographers. Each photo is credited beneath it and in the full photo credits list. Licences used: CC-BY-NC (10,496), CC-BY (1,349), CC-BY-NC-SA (197), CC0 (155), CC-BY-SA (93).

Some iNaturalist species are so alike that they cannot be told apart from a photo (for example several newly separated vine snakes). Those are grouped as a single species here, and the photo’s original iNaturalist name is shown in its credit line.

Some licences are "NonCommercial", so this site must stay non-commercial unless the data set is rebuilt with only commercial-friendly licences (the README explains how). Indian state names are derived from each photo’s location using OpenStreetMap-based boundaries © OpenStreetMap contributors; US state and Canadian province names use Natural Earth’s public-domain boundaries.

Accuracy and limits

Species descriptions are written for general education from standard field-guide knowledge. Identification on iNaturalist is community-based and a few photos may be mislabelled, and snakes vary a lot in colour and pattern. If you spot a mistake, use the "Report photo" button on a question, or open an issue.

Do not use this app to decide whether a real snake is dangerous. Treat every wild snake as potentially dangerous and keep your distance.

Privacy

No adverts, no cookies and nothing that follows you around: visits are only counted with cookie-free statistics. Your progress, badges and settings are stored in your own browser. If you make an account (just a username and a 6-digit PIN, stored only as a one-way hash), your progress is also saved with it so it follows you to other devices, and your username and scores appear on the leaderboards. You can delete the account, and everything saved with it, at any time.

Details are in the privacy policy and the terms of use.

Open source

The code is open source (MIT licence). The README explains how the photo data set is built from iNaturalist’s open data and how to rebuild or extend it. View the project on GitHub.
