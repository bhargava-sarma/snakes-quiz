Common Krait

Bungarus caeruleus

Also called Indian Krait, Blue Krait

Highly venomousBig FourIndiaElapidaeDifficulty

How to identify

A Common Krait

Medically important: its bite can kill without urgent hospital treatment.

  • Glossy blue-black or blackish-brown body crossed by narrow white bands, often in pairs, that are clearest on the rear half. Some adults are almost plain.
  • A row of enlarged six-sided (hexagonal) scales runs down the middle of the back. No other common snake has this.
  • Flat-topped, rounded head only slightly wider than the neck, with a blunt snout and small dark eyes.
  • Body is slightly triangular in cross-section with a ridged back; the tail is short and blunt.
  • Plain white belly. Often coils with its head tucked under the body when disturbed.

Did you know? Kraits are docile by day but kill many people because they slip into homes at night and bite sleepers, often so painlessly that the victim does not wake. Sleeping off the floor and under a bed-net is real protection.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Common Wolf SnakeLycodon aulicus

A krait has a row of enlarged six-sided scales along its spine and a plain blunt head with tiny eyes. A wolf snake has ordinary scales down the back, a flat head wider than its neck and big eyes with vertical pupils. Wolf snakes are harmless.

Harmless

Barred Wolf SnakeLycodon striatus

Both are glossy black with white bars, which is why wolf snakes are so often killed as kraits. The Barred Wolf Snake has crisp bars mostly on the neck and front, a flat head wider than its neck and big eyes. The Common Krait has thin paired bands strongest on the rear half, a blunt head hardly wider than its neck and a row of enlarged six-sided scales on its back.

Harmless

Travancore Wolf SnakeLycodon travancoricus

The Travancore Wolf Snake has a flat head wider than its neck, big eyes and speckled, chain-like pale bands. The Common Krait has a blunt head that barely widens behind the eyes, tiny eyes and a row of enlarged six-sided scales along the spine. The wolf snake is harmless.

Harmless

Greater Black KraitBungarus niger

The Common Krait is blue-black with narrow white bands, clearest on the rear half. The Greater Black Krait is plain glossy black (sometimes with faint pale bands) and is found only in the north-east, while the Common Krait is widespread across India.

Deadly venom

Quick facts

Length
0.9-1.2 m, up to about 1.75 m
Habitat
Farmland, villages, houses, scrub and places near water; hides in rodent holes, termite mounds, rubble and wood piles
Found in
Most of India (rarer in the north-east and high Himalaya), Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka
Eats
Other snakes, including venomous ones, plus lizards, frogs and small rodents
Active
Strictly nocturnal; sluggish and docile by day
Family
Elapidae (cobras, kraits, coral & sea snakes)
