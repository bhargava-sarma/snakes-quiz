King Cobra

Ophiophagus hannah

Also called Hamadryad

Highly venomousIndiaElapidaeDifficulty

© Girish Gowda (@girishgowda)·CC BY-NC·iNaturalist·Karnataka·iNat ID: Ophiophagus kaalinga

How to identify

A King Cobra

Medically important: its bite can kill without urgent hospital treatment.

  • Enormous size: a long, powerful snake, often 3 m or more, that can raise a third of its body off the ground.
  • Spreads a long, narrow hood that has no spectacle mark; the hood is more slender than an Indian Cobra's.
  • Two extra large scales (occipitals) at the back of the head, a feature only king cobras have.
  • Adults are olive, brown or blackish with faint pale cross-bands and a cream or yellow throat; the young are glossy black with bold white or yellow bands.
  • Growls: a low, deep hiss like a dog's growl.

Did you know? The king cobra is the only snake that builds a nest: the female heaps leaves into a mound for her eggs and guards them until they hatch.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Indian CobraNaja naja

A king cobra is far larger (3 m and more), has a longer, narrower hood with no spectacle mark, and has two extra large scales behind the crown of the head.

Deadly venom

Indian Rat SnakePtyas mucosa

A king cobra spreads a long narrow hood, is much heavier and usually well over 3 m, and growls. The Indian Rat Snake is slimmer, brown with a netted rear half, cannot make a hood and is rarely longer than about 2.5 m.

Harmless

Monocled CobraNaja kaouthia

The Monocled Cobra has a plain, unmarked body, whereas the King Cobra has distinct cross-bands along the body.

Deadly venom

Large-eyed False CobraPseudoxenodon macrops

The Large-eyed False Cobra is medium-sized (roughly 0.8-1.5 m), whereas the King Cobra is a giant (well over 2.5 m).

Quick facts

Length
3-4 m; the longest venomous snake in the world (to about 5.5 m)
Habitat
Dense evergreen and moist forest, bamboo thickets and plantations, usually near streams
Found in
Western Ghats, forests of Odisha and the Eastern Ghats, the Himalayan foothills and north-east India (the Western Ghats snakes are sometimes treated as a separate species)
Eats
Mostly other snakes (rat snakes, cobras, pythons), sometimes monitor lizards
Active
Mainly by day
Family
Elapidae (cobras, kraits, coral & sea snakes)
