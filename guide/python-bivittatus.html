Burmese Python

Python bivittatus

Non-venomousIndia & North AmericaPythonidaeDifficulty

How to identify

A Burmese Python

Non-venomous.

  • A very large, heavy constrictor with a pattern of large, rich brown, dark-edged blotches with a light centre on a paler tan or grey background (the pattern is darker and more contrasted than the rock python's).
  • A spear-shaped, dark arrow mark on top of the head, and a dark streak from the eye to the jaw.
  • Heat-sensing pits along the lip scales; smooth scales and a short, blunt tail.
  • Replaces the Indian Rock Python in the north-east and parts of the Himalayan foothills.

Did you know? Burmese Pythons are among the largest snakes of the subcontinent. They are protected by law, and in the wild they usually avoid people.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Indian Rock PythonPython molurus

The Burmese Python is bigger, with darker, more contrasting brown blotches that have pale centres, and lives in the north-east and the Himalayan foothills. The Indian Rock Python has paler, more irregular blotches and lives in the rest of the country.

Harmless

Royal SnakeSpalerosophis atriceps

The Royal Snake has a narrow head barely wider than its neck, whereas the Burmese Python has a head clearly broader than its neck.

Harmless

GophersnakePituophis catenifer

The Gophersnake has a long, pointed snout, whereas the Burmese Python has a blunt, rounded snout.

Harmless

Bella Rat SnakeArchelaphe bella

The Bella Rat Snake has a narrow head barely wider than its neck, whereas the Burmese Python has a head clearly broader than its neck.

Quick facts

Length
3-5 m, occasionally more
Habitat
Wet forest, grassland, swamps, river banks and farmland in the plains and foothills, close to water
Found in
North-east India, West Bengal, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh terai and Uttarakhand foothills; also Nepal, Bangladesh and South-East Asia. Introduced in Florida, where it is now established in the Everglades.
Eats
Birds, rodents, monkeys, deer and pigs
Active
Mostly nocturnal
Family
Pythonidae (pythons)
