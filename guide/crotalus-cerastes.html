How to identify

A Sidewinder

Venomous: a bite needs urgent medical care.

  • A small, pale rattlesnake with a raised, horn-like scale above each eye.
  • Moves by "sidewinding", throwing its body sideways and leaving a row of J-shaped tracks in the sand.
  • Cream, tan, pink or grey to match the sand, with faint darker blotches.
  • A rattle on the tail, and a triangular head with vertical pupils.

Did you know? Sidewinding lets it cross loose, hot sand with only two short parts of its body touching the ground at a time.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Saw-scaled ViperEchis carinatus

They live on different continents, though both sidewind across sand. The Saw-scaled Viper (South Asia) has no rattle and a white cross or "bird's foot" mark on its head; the Sidewinder (North America) has a rattle and a horn-like scale over each eye.

Quick facts

Length
0.45-0.75 m (18-30 in)
Habitat
Sandy desert with dunes, creosote flats and washes
Found in
The Mojave and Sonoran Deserts: south-eastern California, southern Nevada, south-western Utah and western Arizona; into Mexico
Eats
Lizards, kangaroo rats and pocket mice
Active
Mostly at night; by day it buries itself in sand under bushes
Family
Viperidae (vipers & pit vipers)
