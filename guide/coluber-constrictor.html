North American Racer

Coluber constrictor

Also called Black Racer, Blue Racer, Yellow-bellied Racer, Southern Black Racer

Non-venomousNorth AmericaColubridaeDifficulty

How to identify

A North American Racer

Non-venomous.

  • Long, slim and very fast, often holding its head high as it hunts.
  • Adults are plain: black in the east, blue-grey in the Midwest, olive, brown or grey in the west, with a paler chin and throat.
  • Large eyes and smooth, satiny scales.
  • Young are grey with a row of red-brown blotches, quite unlike the adults, and change colour in their second year.

Did you know? Despite the scientific name it does not constrict; it simply overpowers prey and swallows it alive.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Eastern RatsnakePantherophis alleghaniensis

Both can be black with a white chin. The racer is slimmer and faster, with smooth, satiny scales and a round body; the Eastern Ratsnake is bulkier, with a flat belly, a squarish cross-section, faint traces of blotches and a checkered belly.

Harmless

CoachwhipMasticophis flagellum

The Coachwhip is even longer and thinner, dark in front fading to tan behind, with braided-looking tail scales. The racer is evenly coloured from head to tail.

Harmless

Quick facts

Length
0.9-1.5 m, rarely 1.8 m (3-5 ft)
Habitat
Fields, meadows, open woodland, brushy edges, dunes and suburbs
Found in
Most of the USA and southern Canada (Ontario, British Columbia and the prairies), except the driest south-west; into Mexico and Central America
Eats
Insects, frogs, lizards, rodents, birds and other snakes
Active
By day; very active hunter
Family
Colubridae (colubrids (rat snakes, racers, kingsnakes, garter & water snakes ...))
