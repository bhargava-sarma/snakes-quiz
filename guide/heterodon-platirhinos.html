Eastern Hog-nosed Snake

Heterodon platirhinos

Also called Puff Adder, Spreading Adder, Hognose Snake

Mildly venomousNorth AmericaColubridaeDifficulty

How to identify

An Eastern Hog-nosed Snake

Mildly venomous (rear-fanged): not considered dangerous to people.

  • A thick-bodied snake with an upturned, shovel-like snout.
  • Very variable colour: yellow, tan, grey, olive, orange or all black, usually with dark blotches.
  • When threatened it flattens its neck like a cobra, hisses loudly, and then rolls over and plays dead with its mouth open.
  • Keeled scales; the underside of the tail is paler than the belly.

Did you know? Its rear teeth and mild saliva help it deal with toads; it almost never bites people, preferring to play dead.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Eastern CopperheadAgkistrodon contortrix

The Eastern Hog-nosed Snake has an upturned snout, round pupils and often flattens its neck and plays dead. The Copperhead has a plain, pointed-oval head, vertical pupils and hourglass bands.

Venomous

Eastern MassasaugaSistrurus catenatus

The Eastern Hog-nosed Snake has an upturned snout and round pupils and no rattle; the Massasauga has a rattle, vertical pupils and a dark stripe through the eye.

Venomous

Pygmy RattlesnakeSistrurus miliarius

The Pygmy Rattlesnake has a tiny rattle, vertical pupils and often an orange stripe down the spine; the Eastern Hog-nosed Snake has an upturned snout, round pupils and no rattle.

Venomous

Indian CobraNaja naja

They live on different continents. The Spectacled Cobra (South Asia) rears up and spreads a wide hood, often with a spectacle mark; the Eastern Hog-nosed Snake (North America) flattens its neck while staying low, has an upturned snout, and then plays dead.
