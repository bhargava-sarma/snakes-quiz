Coachwhip

Masticophis flagellum

Also called Eastern Coachwhip

Non-venomousNorth AmericaColubridaeDifficulty

© Neil Balchan (@neilbalchan)·CC BY-NC·iNaturalist·Oklahoma

How to identify

A Coachwhip

Non-venomous.

  • Extremely long and slender, with a long, thin tail.
  • In the east the front of the body is dark brown or black, fading to tan or reddish towards the tail.
  • The tail scales look braided, like the plaited leather of a whip.
  • Large eyes, smooth scales and a narrow head; it races off at the slightest disturbance.

Did you know? Old stories say a coachwhip chases people and whips them with its tail; it does neither.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

North American RacerColuber constrictor

The Coachwhip is even longer and thinner, dark in front fading to tan behind, with braided-looking tail scales. The racer is evenly coloured from head to tail.

Harmless

Black KingsnakeLampropeltis nigra

The Black Kingsnake has a body of medium thickness, whereas the Coachwhip has a very thin, whip-like body.

Quick facts

Length
1.2-1.8 m, rarely 2.6 m (4-6 ft)
Habitat
Open sandy country: sandhills, pine scrub, prairie, desert grassland and old fields
Found in
Southern USA from North Carolina and Florida west to Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas and New Mexico; into Mexico
Eats
Lizards, snakes, rodents, birds and large insects
Active
By day, even in the heat
Family
Colubridae (colubrids (rat snakes, racers, kingsnakes, garter & water snakes ...))
