Greater Black Krait

How to identify

A Greater Black Krait

Medically important: its bite can kill without urgent hospital treatment.

  • A glossy, jet-black or very dark brown snake with a plain, unmarked body and a white or pale chin and belly, sometimes with faint pale flecks or narrow pale bands on the rear.
  • A row of enlarged six-sided scales runs down the back, like other kraits.
  • A flat, rounded black head barely wider than the neck and small eyes; a short tail.
  • Easily mistaken for a harmless black snake, but it is a deadly krait.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Common KraitBungarus caeruleus

The Common Krait is blue-black with narrow white bands, clearest on the rear half. The Greater Black Krait is plain glossy black (sometimes with faint pale bands) and is found only in the north-east, while the Common Krait is widespread across India.

Deadly venom

Monocled CobraNaja kaouthia

The Monocled Cobra spreads a hood and has a ring mark on its back. The Greater Black Krait is plain glossy black, never spreads a hood and has a pale belly. Both are deadly.

Deadly venom

Andaman KraitBungarus andamanensis

The Andaman Krait has distinct cross-bands along the body, whereas the Greater Black Krait has a plain, unmarked body.

Deadly venom

Northeastern Hill KraitBungarus bungaroides

The Northeastern Hill Krait has distinct cross-bands along the body, whereas the Greater Black Krait has a plain, unmarked body.

Quick facts

Length
0.9-1.3 m, up to about 1.5 m
Habitat
Forest edges, bamboo, tea gardens, paddy fields and villages in damp lowlands and foothills
Found in
North-east India (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Tripura) and Bangladesh
Eats
Other snakes, lizards and frogs
Active
Nocturnal; placid by day
Family
Elapidae (cobras, kraits, coral & sea snakes)
