Gophersnake

Pituophis catenifer

Also called Bullsnake, Pacific Gophersnake, Sonoran Gophersnake, Gopher Snake

Non-venomousNorth AmericaColubridaeDifficulty

© Tom Mills (@tmills)·CC BY-NC·iNaturalist·Arizona

How to identify

A Gophersnake

Non-venomous.

  • A large, heavy snake, yellow, cream or tan with dark brown or black squarish blotches down the back.
  • A dark band across the head between the eyes, and a dark stripe from each eye to the jaw.
  • A narrow head with round pupils and a pointed tail without a rattle.
  • When threatened it hisses loudly, flattens its head and shakes its tail, imitating a rattlesnake.

Did you know? Its rattlesnake act is so convincing that many are killed by mistake; it is a valuable catcher of rodents.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Western RattlesnakeCrotalus oreganus

The Gophersnake copies a rattlesnake by hissing, flattening its head and shaking its tail, but it has a narrow head, round pupils, no heat pits, no rattle and a pointed tail. The Western Rattlesnake has a broad triangular head, vertical pupils and a rattle.

Deadly venom

Prairie RattlesnakeCrotalus viridis

Look at the head and tail: the Bullsnake (a Gophersnake) has a narrow head, round pupils and a pointed tail; the Prairie Rattlesnake has a wide triangular head, vertical pupils, pale face lines and a rattle.

Deadly venom

Western Glossy SnakeArizona occidentalis

The Western Glossy Snake has very smooth, shiny scales, a faded pattern and a plain white belly; the Gophersnake has keeled scales, a dark band between the eyes and a spotted belly.

Harmless

Eastern Hog-nosed SnakeHeterodon platirhinos

The Eastern Hog-nosed Snake has an upturned, hump-like snout, whereas the Gophersnake has a long, pointed snout.

Mild venom

Quick facts

Length
0.9-1.8 m, rarely 2.5 m (3-6 ft)
Habitat
Grassland, prairie, desert scrub, farmland, open woodland and chaparral
Found in
Western and central North America, from British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan to California, the Great Plains and Texas; into Mexico
Eats
Gophers, ground squirrels, mice, rabbits, birds and eggs
Active
By day; at night in hot weather
Family
Colubridae (colubrids (rat snakes, racers, kingsnakes, garter & water snakes ...))
