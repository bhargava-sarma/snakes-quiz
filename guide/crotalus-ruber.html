How to identify

A Red Diamond Rattlesnake

Medically important: its bite can kill without urgent hospital treatment.

  • Brick-red, pinkish or tan, with pale-edged diamonds.
  • A black-and-white ringed tail before the rattle, like the Western Diamondback.
  • Large and heavy, with a broad triangular head.

Did you know? Despite its size it is usually mild-tempered, but its bite is dangerous like any large rattlesnake's.

Look-alikesand how to tell them apart

Western Diamondback RattlesnakeCrotalus atrox

Both have a black-and-white "coon tail". The Red Diamond is brick-red to pinkish and lives in southern California; the Western Diamondback is grey to brown, dusty-looking, and lives from Arizona eastwards.
