Heller's Red-necked Keelback vs Buff-striped Keelback

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Heller's Red-necked Keelback

Rhabdophis helleri
VenomousIndia

Buff-striped Keelback

Amphiesma stolatum
Non-venomousIndia

What sets them apart

Heller's Red-necked Keelback has a red or orange neck and is rear-fanged (treat it as dangerous). The Buff-striped Keelback has two pale stripes along the back, no red neck and is harmless.

  • Pattern. The Heller's Red-necked Keelback has two clearly different colours (back and belly or front and rear); the Buff-striped Keelback has stripes running along the body.

The Buff-striped Keelback is not dangerous, but the Heller's Red-necked Keelback is venomous.

Recognising the Heller's Red-necked Keelback

  • An olive or brownish snake with a bright orange-red or red patch on the neck and forebody, often with black and yellow markings behind the head.
  • Keeled scales, round pupils and a slightly widened head. The red neck is the quickest clue.
  • Rear-fanged: red-necked keelbacks are known to cause serious bleeding after a bite, so they must be treated as dangerous.

Recognising the Buff-striped Keelback

  • Olive-brown to brown snake with two pale yellowish ("buff") stripes running down the back from the neck, with a series of dark spots or cross-bars between and beside them.
  • Yellow or white lips and a pair of dark spots (or streaks) on the sides of the head; yellowish belly.
  • Rough, keeled scales and a fairly short, stout body for its length.
  • Often basks in grass by day and flees quickly. Harmless; sometimes flattens its neck like a cobra.
