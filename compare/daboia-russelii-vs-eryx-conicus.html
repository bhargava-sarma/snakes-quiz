Russell's Viper vs Common Sand Boa

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Russell's Viper

Daboia russelii
Highly venomousBig FourIndia

Common Sand Boa

Eryx conicus
Non-venomousIndia

What sets them apart

Russell's Viper has a big triangular head clearly wider than its neck, large eyes with vertical pupils and rows of ringed oval spots. The Common Sand Boa has a small blunt head with tiny eyes, no neck and a blunt tail that looks like its head. The sand boa is harmless.

  • Snout. The Russell's Viper has a blunt, rounded snout; the Common Sand Boa has a small, wedge-shaped snout for burrowing.
  • Head. The Russell's Viper has a head clearly broader than its neck; the Common Sand Boa has a narrow head barely wider than its neck.
  • Eyes. The Russell's Viper has vertical, cat-like pupils; the Common Sand Boa has round pupils.

The Common Sand Boa is not dangerous, but the Russell's Viper is highly venomous.

Recognising the Russell's Viper

  • Three rows of large dark oval spots with black-and-white rims run down the back and flanks on a tan, brown or yellowish body, like a chain.
  • Broad, triangular head, clearly wider than the thin neck, with a pale V- or X-shaped mark on top.
  • Thick, heavy body with rough, keeled scales that tapers quickly into a short tail.
  • Large eyes with vertical, slit-like pupils and big, obvious nostrils.
  • Coils into an S-shape and gives a long, sustained hiss, one of the loudest of any snake, before striking.

Recognising the Common Sand Boa

  • A thick, blunt-bodied sand-dweller with a very short blunt tail that looks much like its head, so it is called a "two-headed snake".
  • Brown, tan or reddish-brown with large dark blotches (often in two rows) along the back; the belly is cream.
  • Small, flat, wedge-shaped head with tiny eyes and rough, keeled scales.
  • Slow and docile, and kills its prey by constriction. It is harmless but widely, and wrongly, believed to be venomous.
