Western Ribbonsnake vs Common Garter Snake

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Western Ribbonsnake

Thamnophis proximus
Non-venomousN. America

Common Garter Snake

Thamnophis sirtalis
Non-venomousN. America

What sets them apart

The Western Ribbonsnake is slimmer, with a long tail, plain pale lips and usually an orange back stripe; the Common Garter Snake is stockier with barred lips.

  • Habitat. The Western Ribbonsnake is equally at home on the ground and in bushes and trees; the Common Garter Snake lives on the ground.

Recognising the Western Ribbonsnake

  • Very slender, with a long tail about a third of its length.
  • A bright orange or yellow stripe down the back and yellow side stripes on a black or brown body.
  • Plain, unmarked pale lips, and a white mark in front of the eye.
  • Keeled scales and large eyes.

Recognising the Common Garter Snake

  • Three pale stripes, yellow, green or blue, one down the back and one along each lower side, on a dark body.
  • The side stripes sit on the second and third rows of scales above the belly.
  • Often a checkerboard of dark spots between the stripes, and red or orange flecks on the sides in many areas.
  • Keeled scales, a slim body and round pupils.
