Green Rat Snake vs Red-tailed Racer

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Green Rat Snake

Ptyas nigromarginata
Non-venomousIndia

Red-tailed Racer

Gonyosoma oxycephalum
Non-venomousIndia

What sets them apart

Both are big green snakes. The Green Rat Snake has black-edged scales that form dark lines towards the tail and lives in the north-east; the Red-tailed Racer has a plain green body with a contrasting grey, brown or reddish tail and, in India, lives only on the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

  • Pattern. The Green Rat Snake has stripes running along the body; the Red-tailed Racer has two clearly different colours (back and belly or front and rear).
  • Head. The Green Rat Snake has a narrow head barely wider than its neck; the Red-tailed Racer has a head clearly broader than its neck.
  • Habitat. The Green Rat Snake is equally at home on the ground and in bushes and trees; the Red-tailed Racer lives up in bushes and trees.

Recognising the Green Rat Snake

  • Bright to dark green body with a yellow-green or yellowish head and a black line along the edge of each belly scale.
  • Very long, slender and fast, with a long tail; smooth scales and large round eyes.
  • Often shows a pale stripe on each side of the body and a black-edged line at the lower flank.
  • Harmless, though green like a vine snake or green pit viper; large round pupils separate it from both.

Recognising the Red-tailed Racer

  • A large, bright green tree snake with a grey, brown or reddish-brown tail that contrasts with the green body.
  • A long, narrow head with a dark stripe through the eye, and a pale green or yellowish throat and belly.
  • When threatened it inflates its throat and strikes, but it is non-venomous.
