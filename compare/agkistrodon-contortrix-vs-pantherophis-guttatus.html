Eastern Copperhead vs Red Cornsnake

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Eastern Copperhead

Agkistrodon contortrix
VenomousN. America

Red Cornsnake

Pantherophis guttatus
Non-venomousN. America

What sets them apart

The Red Cornsnake has black-edged red saddles, a spear-point mark on its head, a checkered belly and round pupils. The Copperhead has hourglass bands, a plain coppery head and vertical pupils.

  • Pattern. The Eastern Copperhead has distinct cross-bands along the body; the Red Cornsnake has large blotches or saddles down the back.
  • Scales. The Eastern Copperhead has rough, keeled scales; the Red Cornsnake has smooth scales.
  • Eyes. The Eastern Copperhead has vertical, cat-like pupils; the Red Cornsnake has round pupils.

The Red Cornsnake is not dangerous, but the Eastern Copperhead is venomous.

Recognising the Eastern Copperhead

  • Copper, pinkish or tan, with dark hourglass-shaped crossbands: wide on the sides, narrow over the back.
  • A plain, unmarked coppery head with vertical pupils and a heat-sensing pit.
  • Young snakes have a bright yellow-green tail tip.
  • No rattle. Stout body with keeled scales.

Recognising the Red Cornsnake

  • Orange, red or brownish saddle-shaped blotches outlined in black on a paler orange or grey body.
  • A spear-point mark on top of the head reaching between the eyes.
  • A bold black-and-white checkerboard belly, like Indian corn.
  • Round pupils and smooth to weakly ridged scales.
