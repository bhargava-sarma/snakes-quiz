Checkered Keelback vs Buff-striped Keelback

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Checkered Keelback

Fowlea piscator
Mildly venomousIndia

Buff-striped Keelback

Amphiesma stolatum
Non-venomousIndia

What sets them apart

The Checkered Keelback has a dense checkerboard of small dark squares and is nearly always near water. The Buff-striped Keelback has two pale yellowish stripes running down the back with dark spots between them.

  • Pattern. The Checkered Keelback has a checkered pattern; the Buff-striped Keelback has stripes running along the body.
  • Habitat. The Checkered Keelback lives in or beside water; the Buff-striped Keelback lives on the ground.

Recognising the Checkered Keelback

  • Olive, brown or grey body covered in a dense checkerboard of small dark squares and spots.
  • Dark streaks on the head: one runs from the eye down to the lip, another back to the corner of the mouth, often with a dark V behind the head.
  • Rough, keeled scales; rounded snout, round pupils and a head only slightly wider than the neck.
  • Almost always near water and a quick swimmer, often seen with its head above the surface.
  • When cornered it flattens its neck and may be mistaken for a cobra, but it is harmless.

Recognising the Buff-striped Keelback

  • Olive-brown to brown snake with two pale yellowish ("buff") stripes running down the back from the neck, with a series of dark spots or cross-bars between and beside them.
  • Yellow or white lips and a pair of dark spots (or streaks) on the sides of the head; yellowish belly.
  • Rough, keeled scales and a fairly short, stout body for its length.
  • Often basks in grass by day and flees quickly. Harmless; sometimes flattens its neck like a cobra.
