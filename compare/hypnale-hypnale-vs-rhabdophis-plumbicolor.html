Hump-nosed Pit Viper vs Green Keelback

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Hump-nosed Pit Viper

Hypnale hypnale
VenomousIndia

Green Keelback

Rhabdophis plumbicolor
Mildly venomousIndia

What sets them apart

The Green Keelback bluffs like a viper but has round pupils, no heat pit and a tail that tapers, and is usually olive-green. The Hump-nosed Pit Viper is brown, with a hump-like snout, a heat pit between the eye and nostril and vertical pupils.

  • Colour. The Hump-nosed Pit Viper is mostly brown, red and grey; the Green Keelback is mostly green and olive.
  • Snout. The Hump-nosed Pit Viper has an upturned, hump-like snout; the Green Keelback has a blunt, rounded snout.
  • Eyes. The Hump-nosed Pit Viper has vertical, cat-like pupils; the Green Keelback has round pupils.

The Green Keelback is not dangerous, but the Hump-nosed Pit Viper is venomous.

Recognising the Hump-nosed Pit Viper

  • Small, stout snake with an upturned, hump-like tip to the snout. The snub nose gives it a pug-like profile.
  • Brown, reddish-brown, grey or pinkish body with dark triangles or blotches down the back and a pale, mottled belly.
  • Broad triangular head, thin neck, vertical pupils and a heat-sensing pit between the eye and the nostril.
  • Rough, keeled scales; usually found coiled among dead leaves, so it blends in superbly.
  • Easily mistaken for a harmless snake, but its bite causes severe tissue damage and can injure the kidneys.

Recognising the Green Keelback

  • A stout, olive-green or yellow-green snake with a darker network and a pair of dark chevrons or a black bar behind the head.
  • A short, broad head that resembles a viper's; when alarmed it flattens its head and neck and strikes in a way that makes people mistake it for a pit viper.
  • Rough, keeled scales, a short tail and round pupils; the belly is cream or greenish.
  • Mildly venomous (rear-fanged) and almost never a danger to people.
