Florida Cottonmouth vs Banded Watersnake

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Florida Cottonmouth

Agkistrodon conanti
VenomousN. America

Banded Watersnake

Nerodia fasciata
Non-venomousN. America

What sets them apart

In Florida, the Banded Watersnake is often red or orange with crisp bands and round pupils. The Florida Cottonmouth is darker and heavier, with a bold mask and pale lines on the face.

  • Eyes. The Florida Cottonmouth has vertical, cat-like pupils; the Banded Watersnake has round pupils.

The Banded Watersnake is not dangerous, but the Florida Cottonmouth is venomous.

Recognising the Florida Cottonmouth

  • A heavy, dark water snake: brown to black, with faint wide crossbands.
  • A bold dark mask through the eye, often edged by pale lines on the snout, and vertical pupils.
  • Gapes to show its cotton-white mouth when disturbed.

Recognising the Banded Watersnake

  • Dark crossbands run the whole length of the body, on a grey, tan, red or yellowish background.
  • A dark stripe from the eye to the corner of the jaw.
  • A belly with dark squares or wavy marks.
