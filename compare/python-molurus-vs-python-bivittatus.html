Indian Rock Python vs Burmese Python

How to tell them apart, side by side.

Indian Rock Python

Python molurus
Non-venomousIndia

Burmese Python

Python bivittatus
Non-venomousIN + NA
These two are almost impossible to tell apart from a photograph. Experts rely on where the snake was found and on counting scales.

What sets them apart

The Burmese Python is bigger, with darker, more contrasting brown blotches that have pale centres, and lives in the north-east and the Himalayan foothills. The Indian Rock Python has paler, more irregular blotches and lives in the rest of the country.

Recognising the Indian Rock Python

  • A very big, heavy-bodied constrictor with small smooth scales and a short, tapering tail.
  • Large, irregular dark-edged blotches in pale brown, olive or yellowish on a tan or grey background; the belly is white or cream.
  • Spear-shaped pale mark on top of the head and a dark stripe from the eye to the jaw. Heat-sensing pits sit along the lip scales.
  • Moves slowly and heavily in a straight line and does not spread a hood.

Recognising the Burmese Python

  • A very large, heavy constrictor with a pattern of large, rich brown, dark-edged blotches with a light centre on a paler tan or grey background (the pattern is darker and more contrasted than the rock python's).
  • A spear-shaped, dark arrow mark on top of the head, and a dark streak from the eye to the jaw.
  • Heat-sensing pits along the lip scales; smooth scales and a short, blunt tail.
  • Replaces the Indian Rock Python in the north-east and parts of the Himalayan foothills.
