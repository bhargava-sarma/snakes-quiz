1. About Sarpa

Sarpa is a free website for learning to identify the snakes of India and North America (the USA and Canada): a photo quiz, the Herpdle clue game, a field guide, flashcards and snakebite first-aid information. It is a non-commercial hobby project, made and run in spare time by one person, bhargava-sarma. It has no adverts, no paid features and no payments of any kind, and it does not make money.

By using Sarpa you agree to these terms. If you do not agree, please do not use it.

2. Using Sarpa

You are welcome to use Sarpa for free, for your own learning, in classrooms and in nature education. Please do not use its photos commercially (see section 7), and do not try to break, overload or misuse the website or its accounts.

3. Accounts

  • You can use the field guide, flashcards, safety pages and everything else without an account. Playing the games (Sarpa and Herpdle) needs a free account, so that every game can count on the leaderboards.
  • When you are signed in, your progress (snake-dex, badges, quiz history and daily results) is saved with your account so that it follows you to your other devices.
  • An account is just a username and a 6-digit PIN. Keep your PIN secret. There is no e-mail address on an account, so a forgotten PIN cannot be reset.
  • One person per account. Do not share or sell accounts, and do not sign in to someone else’s.
  • Your username is shown publicly on the leaderboards, so choose a respectful one and do not use your full real name. Usernames that are offensive, hateful, misleading or that pretend to be someone else may be removed.
  • You can delete your account at any time from your profile page.

4. Children

If you are under 18, you may create an account only if a parent or guardian agrees. When you create an account you confirm that you are 18 or older, or that your parent or guardian has agreed. Sarpa shows no adverts and does not track or profile anyone.

5. Fair play

Leaderboards are for fun and only work if everyone plays fairly. Do not use scripts, bots, other people’s answers or anything else that gets around the rules. Results or accounts that break these rules may be removed without notice.

6. Not a safety tool

Sarpa is for learning only. Do not use it to decide whether a real snake is dangerous, and never touch, catch or corner a wild snake. Telling snakes apart from a photo is unreliable, even for experts. A snakebite is always a medical emergency: call 108 or 112 in India, or 911 in the USA and Canada, and go to a hospital. See the safety disclaimer.
