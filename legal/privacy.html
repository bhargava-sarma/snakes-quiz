Privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026.

TermsPrivacyDisclaimerLicencesTakedown

Short version: Sarpa collects as little as it can. Your progress is kept in your own browser. If you make an account, the site stores your username, a scrambled form of your PIN, your game results and a copy of your progress (so it follows you to your other devices), and shows your username and scores on public leaderboards. Visits are counted with cookie-free statistics that cannot follow you from day to day or across websites. No adverts, no cookies, and your data is never sold.

Who is responsible

Sarpa is a non-commercial hobby project run by bhargava-sarma, who decides how its data is used (in legal terms, the data fiduciary or controller). Questions and requests: the project’s issue tracker on GitHub. Issues there are public, so never post your PIN or anything private.

What is kept on your device

Your quiz history, snake-dex, badges, Herpdle games and settings are kept in your browser’s local storage, on your device. Without an account they are not sent anywhere. If you sign in, your username and a random sign-in key are kept there too, along with any finished games still waiting to be saved, and your progress is also saved with your account (see below). Your settings, the list of photos you have already seen and the game you are in the middle of are not part of that copy.

Sarpa used to be at snakes-quiz.vercel.app. If you open that old address, your browser brings your sign-in, settings and progress across to www.sarpa.live inside the link itself (in the part after "#", which browsers never send to any server), and the new address removes it from the link straight away.

On your profile page you can download a backup or erase your progress (“Download backup”, “Reset progress”; when you are signed in, a reset also clears the copy saved with your account). Signing out first saves your progress to your account, then removes it and the sign-in key from this browser. Clearing your browser’s data for this site removes everything stored on the device.

When you visit

  • Hosting. Sarpa is hosted by Vercel. As with any website, Vercel’s servers receive your IP address and basic browser details with every request, to deliver the pages and protect the site, and may keep them for a short time in technical logs.
  • Visitor statistics. Vercel Web Analytics counts visits. For each page view it records the page address, the website that linked to it, your approximate location (country, region and city, worked out from your IP address; the address itself is not recorded with it), and your browser, operating system and type of device. It uses no cookies and stores nothing on your device. Visitors are told apart only by a code made from the request, which is thrown away after 24 hours, so nobody can follow you from day to day or across websites. Only the totals are looked at.
  • Speed measurements. Vercel Speed Insights measures how quickly pages load and respond, together with the page address, your browser, operating system, type of device, network speed (such as “4g”) and country. These measurements are not linked to you or to your IP address.
  • Photos. The photos are served from this site. If your browser cannot show one (for example an older browser without support for the AVIF image format), it loads that photo from iNaturalist’s public image store on Amazon Web Services instead, which then receives your IP address and browser details, as any website would.
  • Links. Links to other websites (iNaturalist, Creative Commons, GitHub) take you to those sites, whose own privacy policies apply.

If you make an account

The accounts database stores only this:

  • Your username, and when you created the account and last changed its PIN. Your username is public: it appears on the leaderboards. Search engines are asked not to read the leaderboards (robots.txt keeps them away from the accounts API, and the copies of the pages made for them leave the leaderboards out), so usernames should not show up in search results.
  • Your PIN, in scrambled form only. It is stored as a salted one-way hash (scrypt), so nobody can read your PIN from the database, including the person who runs the site.
  • Your games: for each ranked game, the date, the game and mode, the points, how many answers were right or how many guesses you needed, and how long it took. From these come your streaks, totals and leaderboard places; a list of your last 50 games is kept for your profile page. The answers you sent are checked and then thrown away.
  • Your progress: a copy of what you have learned, so that it follows you to every browser and device you sign in on: how many times you have answered each species and how often correctly, your totals and best streak, which snakes you mixed up, your last 60 quizzes, your badges, your daily challenge results, your Herpdle games and the photos you reported. Each device’s share is filed under a random device code that says nothing about the device. It is only ever given back to your own account.
  • Sign-in sessions: only a one-way hash of each sign-in key. A session ends when you sign out, when you change your PIN (on your other devices), or after 180 days without use.

Security records

To stop people guessing PINs or flooding the site with accounts, the server counts recent attempts for each account and for each network. A network is recorded only as a one-way code made from its IP address with a secret key, never as the address itself, and these counters delete themselves within two days.

Who handles the data

The website runs on Vercel, and the accounts database on Upstash (connected through Vercel). They store and carry the data only so that the site can run, under their own security and privacy terms, and their servers may be outside India. Nobody else receives it: your data is never sold, never shared for advertising and never used to profile you.

How long it is kept

  • Daily leaderboards: about 140 days. Weekly leaderboards: about 400 days.
  • Your account, statistics, saved progress and all-time leaderboard places: until you delete your account (or, for your progress, reset it), or until the site closes. Accounts are not deleted for being unused.
  • Unfinished games: up to two days. Sign-in sessions: up to 180 days after you last used them.

Your choices and rights

  • No account needed: the field guide, flashcards, safety pages and everything except the ranked games work without one.
  • See your data: your profile page shows your account, statistics, recent games and progress, and “Download backup” gives you a copy of your progress as a file.
  • Delete it: “Delete account” on your profile page removes your username, PIN, sessions, statistics, saved progress and leaderboard places straight away. Only short-lived technical records (unfinished games and attempt counters) are left until they expire, within two days, and your username becomes free for others. This cannot be undone.
  • Ask: for anything else, such as a correction, a question or a complaint, contact the project’s issue tracker on GitHub. Issues there are public, so never post your PIN or anything private.

Data protection laws, such as India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, Canada’s privacy laws and the laws of some US states, may give you further rights, including to access, correct and erase your personal data and to have a grievance dealt with. Ask, and your request will be handled as quickly as possible. In India you can also take a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India, and in Canada to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Children

Children under 18 may create an account only with the agreement of a parent or guardian. Sarpa collects no more from children than from anyone else (a username, a scrambled PIN, game results and progress), and never shows them adverts, tracks them or profiles them. A child or a parent can delete the account from its profile page at any time, or ask for it to be removed through the project’s issue tracker on GitHub (give only the username).

What Sarpa never does

  • No adverts and no advertising trackers.
  • No cookies: Sarpa sets none. (Your browser’s local storage is used only for the things listed above.)
  • No e-mail address, phone number, real name, location or contacts are asked for.
  • Your data is never sold or passed on, except to the hosting providers named above, who run the site.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date.
